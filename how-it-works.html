How WHO works

A simple explanation of what happens when WHO helps identify a caller.

1. A call arrives

WHO receives the operating-system event required to provide caller identification and checks the available local and service-side signals.

2. Signals are compared

Where an identity signal exists, WHO can compare normalized phone identifiers against candidate names, business information, reputation signals or your own contacts.

3. Confidence is calculated

Multiple consistent observations can increase confidence. Conflicting signals should remain visible as uncertainty instead of being silently collapsed into one “verified” name.

4. You stay in control

You can save, correct, report or ignore an identity suggestion. Privacy controls will let you manage your participation as the service evolves.

Important: a community suggestion is not automatically a legal identity verification.

Future community matching

When this feature is launched, WHO intends to use explicit product permissions and a privacy-first data model. We will document the feature and its data use before enabling it.
