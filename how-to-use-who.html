How to use WHO

A practical guide to the WHO Beta 1.0 experience, from first launch to caller information, contacts, SMS, permissions and feedback.

Start here

1. Complete the first launch

When WHO opens for the first time, read the Beta 1.0 welcome message and review the permissions the app requests. Only grant permissions you are comfortable using for the features you need.

2. Set up WHO as your phone app

On supported Android versions, make WHO your default phone/dialer app if you want WHO to handle calling and its related interface. You can change your default app again from Android settings.

3. Use Caller ID

When an unfamiliar number calls, WHO may show available identity or reputation context. Treat the result as useful information, not automatic proof of who the caller is.

4. Save or correct a number

If the number is someone you know, save it to your contacts. When a WHO identity suggestion is incorrect, use the available correction/report controls or the feedback page.

5. Use SMS

Keep your SMS conversations close to your contacts and calling experience. Spam controls and additional message intelligence will expand as the beta develops.

6. Relay & hotspot warning

If WHO asks you to turn off a normal Android hotspot before starting Relay, follow the warning. WHO should not automatically disable your hotspot; use the provided Android settings action instead.

7. Keep WHO updated

WHO may occasionally require an update for security or compatibility. When a mandatory update is configured, the app will guide you to the current release before continuing.

8. Back up contacts

When Google Drive backup is available, choose the backup action in WHO and authorize Google. Backups are intended for your own contacts and are separate from the public caller-intelligence system.

Quick tips

Tip 01

Never share an OTP or banking PIN with an unknown caller, even when WHO shows a familiar-looking name.

Tip 02

Keep WHO updated so your caller, Relay and system integrations stay compatible.

Tip 03

Tell us what is confusing. Early beta feedback directly shapes the next WHO releases.
