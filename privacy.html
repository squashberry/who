Privacy by
architecture.

WHO should make the user more informed about communication, not less informed about how their data is handled.

What WHO is designed to do

WHO uses communication context to help a person decide what to do with calls, messages and connections. Caller intelligence is a confidence-based signal, not proof of legal identity.

Contacts and backups

Your private address book should not silently become a public directory. Backup and restore are user-controlled operations and should remain separate from caller-intelligence data systems.

Community signals

Community intelligence should favor quality, moderation, independent signals, corrections and visible uncertainty rather than raw volume.

Website analytics

The public website may use first-party analytics for page views and download events. The implementation should match the published privacy explanation and should not describe the site as analytics-free when analytics are enabled.

Security

Credentials, service-role keys, database passwords, OAuth client secrets, signing keys and other privileged secrets belong on the backend or secure release systems. They do not belong in the public website.

Changes

This is the rebuilt site's concise privacy surface. The policy should be expanded with the final production data map, legal basis, retention periods, processor list and user-rights workflow before a public release outside beta.
